# Hill Valley County Appraiser's Office
## Policy 7 — Qualification of Sales for Use in Valuation

**Effective:** January 1, 2025
**Owner:** Chief Deputy Appraiser, Valuation

> ⚠️ **Fictional policy, written for an IAAO conference demonstration.**
> Modeled on common practice; not the law anywhere.

---

### 7.1 Why sales are qualified

The Clerk of Courts records every instrument that transfers an interest in real
property. Most of those instruments are **not** evidence of market value. A
deed between family members, a foreclosure, or a transfer settling an estate
tells us who owns the property now, but it does not tell us what a willing buyer
would pay a willing seller.

Using unqualified sales in a ratio study will make the office's performance look
either far better or far worse than it is. Qualification is therefore performed
before any statistical measure is computed — not after.

---

### 7.2 Presumptively qualified instruments

The following deed types are presumed to represent an arm's length transaction
and are used unless staff identifies a specific disqualifying condition:

- **Warranty Deed**
- **Grant Deed**

---

### 7.3 Presumptively disqualified instruments

The following are excluded from all ratio studies and from comparable sales
analysis unless individually verified and documented:

| Deed type | Why it is excluded |
|---|---|
| **Quit Claim Deed** | Conveys whatever interest the grantor has, often none. Commonly used between relatives or to clear title. |
| **Sheriff's Deed** | Forced sale under court order. Not a willing seller. |
| **Executor's Deed** | Estate settlement. Price often reflects an appraisal or a family arrangement rather than the market. |
| **Interfamily Transfer** | Gift or nominal consideration. Frequently recorded at $1, $10, or $100. |

---

### 7.4 Additional disqualifying conditions

Even a Warranty Deed is disqualified when any of the following is present:

1. **Nominal consideration.** Any sale price under $1,000 is treated as a
   non-market transfer regardless of deed type.
2. **Related parties.** Grantor and grantee share a surname or a business
   entity, absent evidence of an arm's length negotiation.
3. **Portfolio or bulk sale.** Multiple parcels conveyed on one instrument for
   a single undivided price.
4. **Sale within 12 months of a prior sale** of the same parcel, where the two
   prices differ by more than 25%. One of the two is not a market sale.
5. **Substantial change between sale and assessment date.** New construction,
   demolition, fire, or a split. The property sold is not the property assessed.
6. **Personal property included** and not separately stated, where the amount is
   material — commonly furnished condominiums.

---

### 7.5 Study period

The standard ratio study period is the **24 months ending December 31** of the
year preceding the assessment year. A shorter period may be used where sales
volume permits; a longer period requires written approval from the Chief Deputy
and time adjustment of the sale prices.

---

### 7.6 Minimum sample

No ratio study conclusion shall be published for any stratum containing fewer
than **five qualified sales**. Strata below that threshold are reported as
"insufficient sales" and are reviewed by other means, including appraiser
inspection and cost approach reconciliation.

Where a neighborhood repeatedly falls below the minimum, it is combined with the
most similar adjacent neighborhood for study purposes, and the combination is
documented in the study workpapers.

---

### 7.7 Documentation

Every disqualification is recorded with a reason code against the instrument
number. The office does not delete unqualified sales; it flags them. A sale that
was disqualified in error can then be recovered, and the record of what was
excluded is available to any party appealing an assessment.
