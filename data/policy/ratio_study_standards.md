# Hill Valley County Appraiser's Office
## Policy 9 — Ratio Study Standards and Corrective Action

**Effective:** January 1, 2025
**Owner:** Chief Deputy Appraiser, Valuation

> ⚠️ **Fictional policy, written for an IAAO conference demonstration.**
> The thresholds below follow published IAAO guidance; the office procedures
> around them are invented.

---

### 9.1 The three questions a ratio study answers

A ratio study compares the assessed value on the roll to the sale price of the
same property. Each qualified sale produces one **ratio**:

    ratio = assessed value ÷ sale price

Three different questions are asked of those ratios, and they are genuinely
different. A neighborhood can pass one and fail another.

| Question | Measure | What failing it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Level** — are we assessing at the right percentage of market value? | Median ratio | Everyone in the neighborhood is assessed too high or too low, together. |
| **Uniformity** — are similar properties treated consistently? | COD | Neighbors in similar houses get wildly different values. Some are right and some are wrong, and you cannot tell which. |
| **Equity** — are modest and expensive homes treated the same? | PRD | One end of the value range is carrying more than its share of the levy. |

---

### 9.2 Level: the median ratio

The **median ratio** is the middle ratio when all qualified sales in the stratum
are sorted. The median is used rather than the mean because it is not moved by a
single extreme sale.

**Standard: 0.90 to 1.10.**

A median below 0.90 means the roll is lagging the market — values have risen and
the assessments have not followed. A median above 1.10 means the opposite.

---

### 9.3 Uniformity: the Coefficient of Dispersion (COD)

COD measures the **average percentage by which a ratio differs from the median
ratio**. Lower is better; a COD of 0 would mean every property in the stratum is
assessed at exactly the same percentage of its value.

    COD = 100 × (average absolute deviation from the median ratio) ÷ median ratio

**Standards:**

| Property type | COD standard |
|---|---|
| Single family residential, improved, newer and homogeneous | 5.0 to 10.0 |
| Single family residential, improved, older or heterogeneous | 5.0 to 15.0 |
| Residential condominium | 5.0 to 20.0 |
| Income producing property | 5.0 to 20.0 |
| Vacant land | 5.0 to 25.0 |

**A COD above 15.0 for improved residential property requires corrective
action under Section 9.6.** A COD below 5.0 is also investigated — it usually
means sales have been used to set values (sales chasing), which invalidates the
study rather than proving excellence.

---

### 9.4 Equity: the Price Related Differential (PRD)

PRD detects whether inexpensive and expensive properties are assessed at
different levels.

    PRD = mean ratio ÷ (total assessed value ÷ total sale price)

**Standard: 0.98 to 1.03.**

- **PRD above 1.03 is regressive.** Lower value properties are assessed at a
  higher percentage of their worth than higher value properties. The owner of a
  modest home pays more than their share. This is the more common failure and
  the more serious one, because it falls hardest on the taxpayers least able to
  absorb it.
- **PRD below 0.98 is progressive.** Higher value properties carry more than
  their share.

PRD is sensitive to a small number of very high value sales. Any PRD outside the
standard is confirmed against the Coefficient of Price Related Bias before
corrective action is taken.

---

### 9.5 Stratification is mandatory

**A countywide ratio study is not sufficient and shall never be reported
alone.** Offsetting errors cancel out in aggregate: a neighborhood assessed
20% low and a neighborhood assessed 20% high produce a countywide median that
looks perfect while both neighborhoods are wrong.

Every study is stratified by, at minimum:

1. Neighborhood
2. Property class
3. Value range — quintiles of sale price

---

### 9.6 Corrective action

When a stratum falls outside a standard in Sections 9.2 through 9.4, the
following applies:

| Finding | Required action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Median ratio outside 0.90–1.10 | Reappraise the stratum; apply a factor only with written approval | Before the next roll is certified |
| COD above the standard | Field review of a sample of at least 20 parcels; correct characteristic data | Within 90 days of the study |
| PRD outside 0.98–1.03 | Review the valuation model for the stratum, specifically the treatment of living area and land at the low end of the value range | Within 90 days of the study |

A stratum failing the same measure in two consecutive study years is escalated
to the Chief Deputy and reported to the County Commission.

---

### 9.7 Reporting

The annual ratio study is published by **March 15** each year and includes,
for every stratum, the sample size, median ratio, COD, PRD, and any corrective
action taken in the prior year. Strata with fewer than five qualified sales are
reported as "insufficient sales" under Policy 7.6 rather than omitted.
